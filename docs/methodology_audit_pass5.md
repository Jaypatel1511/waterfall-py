## HIGH

### HIGH-1 (NEW, self-inflicted): The cash-conservation assertion contradicts the DSRA-draw and reserve-release mechanisms — it raises `WaterfallImbalanceError` in every distress or reserve-release period
- **Section:** `## Validation Tests` — Cash-conservation (waterfall totality) (line 166, **added in this resolution**) vs. `## Waterfall Priority` step 2 (line 78) and `## Reserves` — DSRA Draw / Release / IRA (lines 63, 65, 66).
- **The claim (line 166):** *"each period, the sum of Waterfall Priority steps 1–7 (all outflows plus residual to equity) equals CFADS, within tolerance; `WaterfallImbalanceError` otherwise. This programmatically enforces the 'every dollar of CFADS is assigned to exactly one step' invariant and guards against a future ECF/ladder drift (the pass-4 HIGH-1 class)."* Line 85 restates it: *"a cash-conservation assertion (Validation Tests) verifies steps 1–7 sum to CFADS each period."*
- **Why it's wrong (the identity omits two cash *sources* that the methodology itself defines):** The waterfall has cash inflows other than CFADS. Step 2 (line 78): *"If CFADS is insufficient, **draw the DSRA** to cover the shortfall."* The Reserves Draw rule (line 63): *"when period CFADS is insufficient to cover scheduled debt service, the DSRA is drawn to cover the shortfall (down to zero)."* In such a period the cash **paid** at step 2 equals full scheduled debt service — which is *more* than the CFADS available — with the difference funded by the DSRA draw. So the sum of the step outflows equals **CFADS + DSRA draw**, not CFADS. The correct period identity is:

  **CFADS + reserve draws + reserve releases = Σ(steps 1–7 outflows).**

  The assertion drops the `+ reserve draws + reserve releases` source terms and equates uses to CFADS alone. It is therefore violated by *exactly* the amount of any DSRA draw, any DSRA release at maturity (line 65), and any IRA release on its trigger period (line 66) — i.e., in every distress period and every reserve-release period. The methodology already tracks these movements separately (`reserve_draws` column, line 177; Reserve roll-forward assertion, line 169), which makes the omission from the conservation identity a genuine internal contradiction, not a definitional gray area.
- **Worked failure (a sub-debt-service period — the core use case of a waterfall engine):** CFADS = 30; senior fees = 3; senior scheduled debt service = 40; DSRA opening balance = 20. Step 1 pays fees 3 → 27 remaining. Step 2 needs 40, has 27, **draws 13 from the DSRA**, pays 40. Steps 3–7 receive 0 (no cash left). Σ(steps 1–7 outflows) = 3 + 40 = **43**. CFADS = **30**. |43 − 30| = 13 = the DSRA draw ≫ tolerance → the engine raises **`WaterfallImbalanceError`** and refuses to produce output — on the precise scenario (distress, coverage < 1.0x) the tool is built to model. To make the assertion pass, an implementer would have to *not* draw the DSRA, which silently disables the pass-2 shortfall mechanism (pass-2's "inert DSRA" finding, re-manufactured). Either branch is wrong.
- **Second failure path (no distress required):** at final maturity the DSRA releases its remaining balance (line 65); on its trigger period the IRA pass-through balance releases (line 66). That released cash is a real inflow that must exit somewhere (to equity / to prepayment). If it flows through steps 6–7, Σ(steps 1–7) > CFADS → false `WaterfallImbalanceError` in the release period even with healthy coverage. (Corollary sub-gap: the ladder never states *where* released reserve cash goes — it is a dangling inflow with no rung. See LOW-1.)
- **Why it's HIGH (self-inflicted, blocks GO, but not CRITICAL):** It is a direct contradiction between two LOCKED sections, introduced by this resolution's own new assertion, and it fires in the engine's core scenario. But (a) it produces a *caught, loud* exception, not silent wrong numbers — the pass-3 CRITICAL corrupted the DSCR a user would trust; this halts the run; loud-fail is strictly safer than silent-corrupt; (b) it is confined to the validation layer and does not touch any coverage numerator; (c) the fix is mechanical (one identity, add two source terms). That is the same severity class pass-4 assigned its own locked-section contradiction (HIGH-1). A stricter reviewer could argue CRITICAL on the grounds that it renders the engine inoperable on all stressed and all maturing deals and re-manufactures the "inert DSRA" defect — the finding gates GO under either label, and I rate it **HIGH**.
- **Direction:** Restate the conservation identity to balance *sources against uses*, not uses against CFADS: **`CFADS + reserve draws (DSRA) + reserve releases (DSRA/IRA) = Σ(steps 1–7 outflows) + closing reserve fundings already inside step 3`**, i.e., `sources = uses`, within tolerance. Equivalently, net the reserve movements: `Σ(steps 1–7) − reserve draws − reserve releases = CFADS`. Keep `WaterfallImbalanceError`, but assert the corrected identity. Then re-verify it holds on (i) a surplus period, (ii) a DSRA-draw period, (iii) a maturity/release period. Do **not** re-lock until all three pass on paper.

## MEDIUM

### MED-1 (NEW): Step 6 bundles three co-rung uses (growth capex, permitted distributions, discretionary reserve top-ups) with no stated allocation rule when retained ECF is insufficient to fund all of them
- **Section:** `## Waterfall Priority` step 6 (line 82) and `## Cash Sweep Mechanics` — Sweep and retained ECF (line 109).
- **The issue:** Step 6 funds *"permitted growth/discretionary capex and permitted equity distributions (and discretionary reserve top-ups)"* from the un-swept `(1 − sweep%) × ECF`. Three distinct uses now share a single rung. The methodology gives no order among them and no rule for the case where the borrower's elected step-6 amounts (each an INPUT under line 151's input-preparer regime) **exceed** the retained ECF available at step 6. Unlike the debt rungs, these are not resolved by seniority — they are co-equal discretionary elections. If the engine funds them in list order until retained ECF is exhausted, that order is undocumented and outcome-determining; if it pro-rates, that is also unstated. The pass-4 resolution collapsed what were previously separate lines (old step 6 = distributions only; growth capex had no rung; discretionary top-ups "ranked with distributions") into one bucket without specifying intra-bucket mechanics — a new ambiguity created by the consolidation.
- **Why MEDIUM not HIGH:** it affects only the split *among junior, discretionary, borrower-elected uses*, all of which sit below the sweep and above residual equity; it cannot mis-allocate cash between debt and equity (the sweep already captured the senior share). But it is a genuine under-specification that two implementers would resolve differently, and it interacts with the (broken) conservation assertion: if step-6 elections are *not* capped at retained ECF, Σ(steps 1–7) overshoots and compounds HIGH-1.
- **Direction:** State (a) that step-6 uses are collectively capped at retained ECF `(1 − sweep%) × ECF`, and (b) the allocation rule when elected amounts exceed the cap — either an explicit sub-order (e.g., contractually-required-first, then discretionary) or pro-rata — and note that beyond the cap the elections are simply not funded that period (rolling to residual/equity at step 7 = 0 net effect).
