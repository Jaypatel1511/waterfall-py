## CRITICAL

### CRIT-1: The canonical CFADS is defined "net of required/contractual reserve funding" — this corrupts DSCR/LLCR/PLCR, contradicts the DSRA draw/replenish rule and the reserve roll-forward assertion, and manufactures false shortfalls
- **Section:** `## Cash Flow Source Assumptions` (line 127–129), `## Reserves` (lines 63–64, 69), `## Covenant Pack` (lines 75–77), `## Cash Sweep Mechanics` (lines 89–91), `## Validation Tests` (line 150).
- **The claim:** Line 127 — *"CFADS = cash available for debt service, computed **net of opex, cash taxes, maintenance capex, AND required/contractual reserve funding** — uniform across CRE and project finance."* Line 128 (PF): *"revenues − opex − cash tax − maintenance capex − **required reserve funding**."* Line 129 (CRE): *"NOI − maintenance capex − TI/LC − **required reserve funding**."* Line 69: *"'required/contractual' reserve funding is **netted inside CFADS**; only discretionary reserve top-ups are deducted in the ECF sweep formula."* This "− required reserve funding" term is **new in this resolution** — the pass-2 doc (`ffb2af9`) defined CFADS as `revenues − opex − tax − maintenance capex` with no reserve term. It was added to make the ECF double-count go away.
- **Why it's wrong (four independent failures, all downstream of one bad edit):**
  1. **It is not "cash available for debt service."** By universal convention CFADS sits **above** the debt-service line *and above all reserve movements*; DSCR = CFADS ÷ debt service measures whether operating cash covers debt service *before* any reserve is funded. Subtracting reserve funding *inside* CFADS ranks reserve funding **ahead of** debt service, which is both economically backwards (a debt-service reserve is funded *below*, not above, the debt it protects) and definitionally self-contradictory ("cash available for debt service" that has already had cash removed for something junior to debt service).
  2. **It understates every coverage metric.** DSCR (line 75), LLCR (line 76), and PLCR (line 77) all take this CFADS as their numerator. With required reserve funding netted out, all three are **understated** versus their standard definitions → the engine reports **false covenant breaches** and seeds the entire default/cash-trap cascade (lines 82–83, 109–116) off understated coverage. The doc calls these formulas "self-defining arithmetic requiring no citation" (line 74) — but the arithmetic is wrong because its input is mis-defined.
  3. **It contradicts the DSRA draw/replenish rule the same resolution added.** Line 64 says required DSRA **replenishment** comes *"from subsequent surplus CFADS, ahead of any equity distribution and ahead of discretionary sweep application, and **behind scheduled senior debt service**"* — i.e., replenishment is a **post-CFADS, below-debt-service waterfall step**. Line 69 says the identical cash flow (required reserve funding, of which DSRA replenishment is the archetype) is *"netted inside CFADS"* — i.e., an **above-debt-service** deduction. The **same dollar of DSRA replenishment cannot be both** above debt service (inside CFADS) and below it (line 64). And the reserve roll-forward assertion (line 150) — *"opening + funding − draws + replenishment = closing, per reserve per period"* — tracks funding and replenishment as **explicit period flows**, which cannot be reconciled with them being silently pre-netted into CFADS where the roll-forward cannot see them.
  4. **It manufactures false shortfalls and simultaneous fund-and-draw.** Line 63 draws the DSRA *"when period CFADS is insufficient to cover scheduled debt service."* Worked period: pre-reserve operating cash = 70, scheduled debt service = 60, required DSRA replenishment = 20. Real waterfall: pay debt service 60 (coverage 70/60 = 1.17x, healthy, **no draw**), replenish DSRA with the residual 10. The doc's netting instead computes CFADS = 70 − 20 = **50** < 60 → **fires a spurious DSRA draw of 10** and reports coverage 50/60 = **0.83x (false breach)** — in a period that actually covered debt service comfortably. The reserve is **funded (−20, netted) and drawn (+10) in the same period**, which is incoherent, and the "shortfall" is an artifact of the definition. This is precisely the spurious-default pathology the operations-only clock (line 9) was introduced to kill, re-created one section over.
  5. **The ECF formula is now wrong under either reading.** ECF (line 90) = `CFADS − debt service − distributions − growth capex − discretionary top-ups`. It does **not** subtract required DSRA replenishment. If required replenishment is genuinely a below-debt-service/above-sweep step (line 64), the ECF omits it and **oversweeps**, starving the reserve that line 64 says must be replenished *first*. If instead replenishment is inside CFADS (line 69), then limbs 1–4 apply. There is no reading of these three sections that is simultaneously consistent.
- **Why it's CRITICAL:** CFADS is the single most load-bearing definition in the document — it feeds DSCR, LLCR, PLCR, ECF, and the DSRA-draw trigger. A wrong CFADS definition propagates to every numeric output and to the covenant/default/sweep cascade. It also directly contradicts two other locked sections (Reserves, Validation Tests), so the doc is internally inconsistent on its core mechanic, not merely imprecise. By the document's own standard (pass-1 rated a wrong *citation* CRITICAL for loss of trust), a wrong *core formula* that produces false breaches is more severe.
- **Direction:** **Remove "required/contractual reserve funding" from the CFADS definition (lines 127–129) and from line 69.** CFADS = revenue − opex − cash tax − maintenance capex (PF) / NOI − maintenance capex − TI/LC (CRE) — reserve *funding is not a CFADS deduction*. Then re-establish the correct waterfall order as an explicit, single, acyclic priority (see MED-1): CFADS → scheduled senior debt service → required reserve funding/replenishment (DSRA, MRA) → mandatory ECF sweep → mandatory prepayments → discretionary sweep → equity distributions. Add the required-reserve-replenishment term back into the ECF formula (it ranks ahead of the sweep). The maintenance-capex netting is fine and standard; **only the reserve-funding term is the defect** — do not also strip maintenance capex. Note that the pass-2 ECF double-count (HIGH-3) must then be solved the correct way: ECF deducts growth capex + *discretionary* top-ups only, while required reserve funding is a *separate, visible* waterfall line above the sweep — not hidden inside CFADS.

## MEDIUM

### MED-1: The period waterfall priority is never stated as a single ordered, acyclic list — DSRA replenishment is positioned only against *discretionary* sweeps and distributions, not against the *mandatory* ECF sweep or mandatory prepayments
- **Section:** `## Reserves` (line 64) + `## Cash Sweep Mechanics` (lines 87, 90, 93) + `## Mandatory Prepayments` (lines 102–106).
- **The issue:** The overall period waterfall exists only as scattered "ahead of / behind" fragments. Line 64 ranks DSRA replenishment "behind senior debt service, ahead of equity distribution and ahead of **discretionary** sweep." But the ECF sweep is the **mandatory** sweep (line 87: *"mandatory (ECF above thresholds)"*), and mandatory prepayments (lines 102–106) are a third mandatory outflow — the doc **never** states where required reserve replenishment ranks relative to the mandatory ECF sweep or to mandatory prepayments. A reviewer cannot reconstruct a single deterministic priority queue, and the fragments cannot be proven acyclic. This is the ordering question the third-pass mandate explicitly asked to verify; it is not fully specified.
- **Direction:** Publish one canonical, ordered, acyclic period waterfall (see CRIT-1 Direction for a candidate ordering) and make every "ahead of / behind" fragment reference a position in it. Add an audit-log line emitting the applied order per period.

### MED-2: The breach label still reads "based on user-supplied inputs" though DSCR breaches depend on engine-computed debt service — pass-2 MED-3 was reworded, not resolved
- **Section:** `## Covenant Pack` (line 83) + `## Language Guardrails` (line 167).
- **The issue:** Line 83 emits every breach as *"a mechanical test result based on **user-supplied inputs**."* Pass-2 MED-3 flagged this exact phrasing as inaccurate: a DSCR breach = CFADS (user-supplied) ÷ **debt service (engine-computed** from rate/day-count/amort). The label attributes a result partly produced by the engine's own math *solely* to user inputs — the specific inaccuracy pass-2 raised survives verbatim. In litigation the label is itself exposure ("your label says user inputs, but the 0.82x came from *your* debt-service computation").
- **Direction:** Reword to "mechanical test result computed by the engine from user-supplied inputs (CFADS, covenant levels) **and engine-computed values (debt service, balances)**," and tie each breach's debt-service figure to the interest-accrual reconciliation assertion (line 149).

### MED-3: Bankruptcy / cross-default / judgment are listed as engine "default categories" without stating they are user-supplied event flags — pass-2 MED-4 residual
- **Section:** `## Default and Remedies` (lines 111–112) + `## Language Guardrails` (line 167).
- **The issue:** The resolution reframed "event of default"/"acceleration" as mechanical test results (lines 83, 114 — genuinely improved). But line 111 lists **bankruptcy, cross-default, judgment** as default categories, and line 112 gives *"bankruptcy 0 days"* grace — implying the engine *acts on* bankruptcy. Bankruptcy/judgment/cross-default are **external legal events the engine cannot determine**; it can only consume a user-supplied event flag. Line 114 says the engine "computes when contractual thresholds are crossed," but these three categories are not threshold computations. The doc never states they are input flags, so the "no legal conclusions" guardrail (line 167) leaks here.
- **Direction:** State explicitly that bankruptcy, cross-default, and judgment defaults are driven by **user-supplied event flags**, not determined by the engine; only DSCR/coverage/payment thresholds are engine-computed.

### MED-4: The operations-only clock does not fully reconcile with the still-required `deal_close_date` and "stub periods at close" / "accrue daily" — a pre-operations date remains wired into accrual wording (pass-2 HIGH-2 residual)
- **Section:** `## Modeling horizon` (line 9) vs. `## Interest Mechanics` (line 44) vs. `## Required vs. Optional Parameters` (line 139).
- **The issue:** Line 9 is emphatic — *"no interest accrual before period 0,"* period 0 = conversion/stabilization. Yet line 139 still requires **both** `deal_close_date` **and** `operations_start_date` as **distinct** inputs, and line 44 says *"stub periods **at close** and final maturity … accrue daily."* The doc never constrains `deal_close_date` relative to period 0. If close = period 0 always, the two required dates are redundant (say so); if close may precede period 0 (the only reason to carry both), then "stub period at close" + "accrue daily" implies accrual from close — reintroducing the pre-operations interest that line 9 forbids and that pass-2 HIGH-2 was about. Source/use tie-out "at close" (line 148) and reserves "funded at close → opening balance" (line 9) are *fine* (point-in-time snapshots); the offending clause is line 44's "stub periods at close."
- **Direction:** Either state `operations_start_date` = `deal_close_date` for in-scope deals (and drop one param), or explicitly confine `deal_close_date` to the close snapshot (source/use + opening balances) and reword line 44 to "stub periods at **operations start (period 0)** and final maturity" so no accrual references a pre-period-0 date.

### MED-5: The two-calendar split leaves the SOFR lookback/observation-shift counting calendar unassigned
- **Section:** `## Interest Mechanics` (lines 38, 43).
- **The issue:** The split is otherwise clean — payment/period dates on the U.S. Federal Reserve calendar (line 41), SOFR fixings on the SIFMA U.S. Government Securities calendar (line 43). But line 38 offers *"reset in advance; **lookback days configurable**"* without stating which calendar the lookback/observation shift counts on. A SOFR lookback counts business days on the **SOFR (SIFMA)** calendar, while the reset *date* it shifts back from is a period boundary on the **Fed** calendar. On a day that is a business day on one calendar and a holiday on the other, the observation window is ambiguous, misaligning floating-rate resets. The very split the resolution introduced creates this seam and does not resolve it.
- **Direction:** State that the lookback/observation shift is counted on the SIFMA U.S. Government Securities calendar (consistent with the fixing), while the reset reference date is the Fed-calendar period boundary — and specify how a divergence between the two is handled.

### LOW-5: IRA is vestigial in an operations-only horizon
- `## Reserves` (line 66). The interest reserve account exists to fund **construction-period** interest — which is explicitly out of scope. In an operations-only model where period 0 = stabilization, IRA is held as an opening balance and "released on stabilization trigger" — i.e., it opens and releases at the same period 0. Its presence is harmless but incoherent; either drop IRA from v0.x or state plainly it is a pass-through opening balance released at period 0.

### LOW-6: Stabilization / lease-up release triggers still reference conditions absent from engine inputs — pass-2 LOW-2 unaddressed
- `## Reserves` (lines 66–67) + `## Output Schema` (line 158). IRA release ("stabilization trigger") and lease-up reserve ("trigger-based") are not reduced to engine-computable quantities, and the period table has no occupancy/lease-up field. State that all triggers must resolve to a user-supplied period index or an engine-computable test (e.g., sustained DSCR), never an un-modeled occupancy input.
